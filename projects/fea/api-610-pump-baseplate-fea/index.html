API 610 (OH2) Pump Baseplate FEA & Dynamic Resonance Optimization

Sub-frame structural redesign of a 2.0 m × 2.0 m process pump skid to eliminate a critical resonance condition at the 2× motor operating harmonic (98.33 Hz), achieving an optimal +11.67% separation margin and a 35.1% deflection reduction.

1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement

Process pump skids operating under API 610 and PIP criteria require rigorous dynamic separation to avoid structural resonance excited by rotating machinery harmonics. In continuous industrial duty, resonance amplification leads to premature bearing failure, mechanical seal leakage, and severe shaft misalignment.

Engineering Objective Resonance Avoidance
Skid Optimization

Redesign and optimize the sub-frame stiffening layout of a 2.0 m × 2.0 m process pump skid to eliminate a critical structural resonance condition occurring at the 2× operating harmonic of a 2950 RPM motor.

Critical Challenge -2.08% Margin
Resonance Proximity

The baseline baseplate design exhibited a fundamental natural frequency of 96.28 Hz, sitting dangerously close (-2.08%) to the 2× motor harmonic (98.33 Hz), creating severe dynamic amplification risks.

Engineered Solution +11.67% Safe
X-Braced Truss Grid

Replaced parallel straight ribs with an engineered diagonal X-braced truss grid beneath equipment pedestals, shifting Mode 1 by +14.05% to 109.81 Hz (+11.67% safe margin) while reducing deflections by 35.1%.

Dynamic Harmonic Context: A 2-pole electric induction motor operating at 2950 RPM has a fundamental 1× running frequency of 49.17 Hz and a secondary 2× misalignment/vibration harmonic of 98.33 Hz. Standard API 610 / PIP practices require a minimum natural frequency separation margin of ±10% to ±20% (exclusion band: 88.50 Hz – 108.16 Hz) to prevent destructive resonance.
